"Some mistakes only teach you the value of a person after losing them."— what I learned, too late
I.

I'm Sorry

Kavya,

I've started this letter a hundred times. Crumpled it. Burned it. Started again. Because some apologies don't know how to exist in words — they live in the silence between breaths, in the moment you reach for someone who's no longer there.

I'm sorry. Not the kind you say to fill a silence. The kind that keeps you awake at 3 a.m. when the rain won't stop and every song sounds like your name.

I'm sorry for the times I made you feel small when you were the tallest thing in my life. For the words I chose when I should have chosen silence. For every moment I made you question your worth when you were everything that made mine real.

I'm sorry for the version of me you had to meet. The careless one. You deserved better — not a better version of me, a different kind of care entirely. And yet, you stayed longer than you should have, and even that I took for granted.

"I didn't lose you in one moment. I lost you in every small moment I stopped choosing you."— the truth I carry
II.

Things I Couldn't Say

I wanted to say "don't go" every single time you walked out that door. I just didn't know how to say it without it sounding like I was asking you to stay for me instead of yourself.

I was scared that if I told you how much you meant to me, you'd realize how much power you had — and I was terrified of needing someone that completely.

There were moments I wanted to hold your hand and say "I see you." Not the version you showed the world. The real one. The one only I ever truly noticed.

You were right. About everything you ever quietly suspected. I just never had the courage to confirm it, because confirmation meant reckoning — and I wasn't ready.

I missed you even when you were right there. Missing someone who's still present, because you knew you were slowly making them leave — that's the cruelest loneliness.

"I love you" felt too small. So I said nothing. The grandest mistake — choosing inadequate silence over imperfect words.

III.

Memories With You

the beginning

I remember the first time you laughed around me — really laughed, the kind that caught you off guard. I thought: this is the sound I want to protect. I didn't protect it nearly well enough.

rainy evenings

Those evenings when the world felt like it existed only inside our conversation. The rain outside, the soft light, you talking about something you cared about — I was only half listening. I'd give anything to go back and listen completely.

the quiet moments

The silences we shared that didn't need filling. That kind of ease is something most people search for their whole lives and never find. I had it and called it ordinary.

your eyes when you were hurt

I knew the exact look. The way your eyes would go quiet before the rest of you followed. I caused that look more times than I can forgive myself for. It haunts me — softly, constantly.

the last time

I didn't know it was the last time. I wish I had. I would have held on longer. Said more. Let you know that even in my worst moments, the best thing about my life was you.

"You were the warmth I walked away from, standing in cold rooms wondering why I was always cold."— the irony I live with
IV.

Late Night Regrets

The night feels longer when the person you want to call is the same person you hurt. So you just sit with it — the weight of what you did and what you didn't.

  • For every time I replied with silence when you needed words.
  • For making you feel like too much, when you were always exactly enough.
  • For the arguments that didn't need to happen if I had just paused.
  • For letting pride win when I should have let you win instead.
  • For the nights you cried that I don't know about — because I wasn't there to ask.
  • For not being the person you believed I could be, when you were already that person for me.
  • For the version of us that existed only in the moments I wasn't ruining it.
V.

If I Could Fix Everything

I know I can't rewrite what happened. But if the universe handed me one quiet moment to do it all again — here is what I would do differently:

1

I would listen — not to respond, but to truly understand the things you were too gentle to say loudly. I would sit in your pain instead of making it smaller so I could feel less guilty.

2

I would choose you. Consciously, daily, loudly. Not just in the big moments but in the small ones — the ones you think no one notices. I would notice them all.

3

I would say the things that lived in my chest without ever making it to my mouth. Loving someone in silence and calling it protection is just another kind of cowardice.

4

I would be softer. Not weaker — softer. There's a difference I only learned from losing you. Softness is the bravest thing.

5

I would tell you plainly: You mattered. You matter still. Not in a past-tense way. In every present moment, in every quiet room, in every song that sounds like the last conversation we never had.

with everything I should have said sooner —

Kavya.

This isn't a goodbye. It's just the truth, finally sitting still long enough to be written. Whatever you choose — I hope you choose yourself first. You always deserved that the most.
